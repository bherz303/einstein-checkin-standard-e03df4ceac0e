How to run a one-on-one check-in

In five weeks you have completed 336 one-on-ones with 178 movers — a conversation that did not exist as a process before August. That is the win, and I want to say it plainly before anything else. The gap is not effort and it is not adoption. It is that a mover who writes I need help stacking the truck deserves an answer, and right now about two thirds of them get a score instead. This is the standard, field by field: what we expect to see in each box, plus real answers from the app on both sides of the line.

Start here — what we found

Mike pulled every check-in written in the app since launch — all 10 branches, 336 completed one-on-ones, 1,680 manager answers. This is what it showed.

THE HEADLINE

We are doing the check-ins. In 42% of the boxes we are not writing anything in them.

The worst field is the one that matters most. Acknowledge their feedback is 63% filler. That box exists to prove you heard what the mover just told you. Two thirds of the time nobody answered it. Movers are writing real things — I need better copilots, stacking the truck, more support on long jobs, more hours — and getting 10/10 back.

The best field is Expectations going forward at 14% filler. It is also the only field in the form that says out loud This should never be left empty. That is not a coincidence, and it is most of what this page is built on.

The managers at the top of the table are not writing more than everyone else. One of them has written 14 check-ins total. They are just answering the question in front of them. This is not a volume problem and it is not a time problem.

Where it stands right now: 95 movers have answered their side and are waiting on a manager — the oldest for 13 days. Two branches hold most of that. If you have people waiting, that is the first thing to clear.

One convention before the examples: every model answer on this page is written to the mover, in the second person, the same way the app’s own built-in examples are. Write it as if he is going to read it back later, because that is the version that holds up if the conversation ever needs to.

The five fields

Each field below gives you four things: the prompt exactly as it appears in your box, what it is actually asking for, a model answer — what we expect to see, and then real answers from the app on both sides of the line. Copy the shape of the model, not the words. The good real examples are credited by name; the weak ones are not, because the point is the pattern and not the person. Every field closes with the one question to check your answer against before you hit complete.

1 · Attendance

FILLER RATE 44%
WHAT THE BOX SAYS Any attendance trends to address? Consistently late? Always calls out on weekends?
Ex. You are currently sitting with X late points and X callout points. You need to work on showing up on time and ready to work.

What it is asking: what you observed, and what happens about it. Two numbers go in this box, not one: the late and callout points, and the mover’s EMC Games attendance score. The points are on the same screen you are typing on, and the Games score is on the games dashboard you are already in every day — so this is a glance, not a research project. Use it, because it is the attendance number that actually costs him something: attendance is one of the scored Games categories and it feeds his total. 7 is baseline, 8–9 is strong, 10 and up is where bonus lives.

◆ WHAT WE EXPECT TO SEE — SOMEONE DRIFTING You are at 3 of 8 late points and 1 of 4 callout points, and your Games attendance score is 6.4 — under the 7 baseline, and right now it is the category pulling your total down. Both of the recent lates were Mondays, so that reads as a pattern rather than one bad morning. Be clocked in by 6:45 on Mondays; get that score back over 7 and this stops being a topic. AND WHEN SOMEONE IS DOING IT RIGHT — SAY THE NUMBER ANYWAY 1 of 8 late points, no callouts, and your Games attendance score is a 9.6 — top third of the branch. You are two tenths off the 10 that puts attendance into bonus territory. Keep the Mondays clean and you get there this season. THE SHAPE · the point totals → the Games attendance score against the 7 baseline → what you observed → what changes going forward
✓ HALF RIGHT — A REAL ONE FROM THE APP Sitting at 1 of 8 late points and 0.5 of 4 callout points, no trend. First late was this morning — going forward the expectation is that this stays to an absolute minimum. Better than most: real point totals, a read on whether it is a trend, and a stated expectation. Under the new standard it is still short one number — no Games attendance score, so the mover has no idea what those lates are costing him on the board.
✗ THIS DOES NOT x/10 — one manager answered attendance this way in 84 of his 88 check-ins. A score on its own is not an observation. The mover cannot tell from a bare number whether he is fine, drifting, or one callout from a conversation — and a number with no scale on it is worse than none, because x/10 is not even the scale the Games run on.

THE TEST — does your answer carry both numbers — the points and the Games attendance score — and would the mover know from it whether he needs to change something tomorrow?

3 · Peer feedback

FILLER RATE 53%
WHAT THE BOX SAYS What has the team been saying this person needs to work on? Anything mgmt needs to point out for them to work on?
Ex. Feedback from the team is that you need to stay off your phone in the field and make sure you are wrapping things fully.

What it is asking: what their crew says about working with them. This field is an instruction to go ask somebody before you sit down. It cannot be answered from your desk.

◆ WHAT WE EXPECT TO SEE Asked your lead and one of the guys you ran with last week. Both said your wrapping has gotten noticeably faster. The one thing that came back was staying off your phone during load-in — nobody called it a problem, but it is the thing to tighten before you get looked at for lead. THE SHAPE · who you asked → what came back, the good and the one thing → what to do with it
✓ THIS WORKS Just need to stay off the phone and show more engagement in morning truck duties. Wraps and moving skills are there; we just need more ownership over results to make you a lead.
✗ THIS DOES NOT Zero negative feedback · N/A · blank. Usually this means nobody was asked. If you genuinely heard nothing, say who you asked and that they had nothing — that is a real answer and it takes six more words.

THE TEST — can you name the person whose feedback you are passing on?

4 · Acknowledge their feedback

FILLER RATE 63% — WORST FIELD
WHAT THE BOX SAYS Did the mover ask any questions or provide any feedback that needs some context or better clarity for them to understand?

What it is asking: the answer to the thing they just asked for. Read that wording again though — it lands like a yes/no question, which is exactly why this field is the worst one in the app. If you have been writing Yes, or writing down what the mover said, you were answering the question as written. We are rewriting the prompt and adding a worked example. In the meantime, treat this box as: quote what they asked, then say what you are doing about it.

If something comes up before the next check-in, come find me. You do not have to wait for this conversation. FILLER RATE 14% — BEST FIELD

The bar — what makes a check-in complete

Five checks. Run them before you hit Mark one-on-one complete. If all five pass, the check-in counts.

1 Five answers, five real sentences. No bare score, no N/A, no dash, no single word, no same as above, no covered in week 1. If it is under about fifteen characters it is not an answer.
2 The acknowledge box answers what the mover actually wrote. Quote it back, then say what you are doing about it. If he asked for nothing, say who you asked and that he had nothing to raise.
3 Expectations names a behavior, a next action, and a date or a consequence. This is the field that has to hold up if the conversation ever becomes a written warning — the question later is always did they know?
4 Nothing is copy-pasted from another mover. If all five fields match somebody else’s word for word, the conversation did not happen. 23 check-ins in the company currently fail this.
5 It happens within two work days of the mover answering. Same window as the day-one check-in on a new hire. Right now 95 movers are waiting, the oldest 13 days.

When a check-in becomes a warning

This is the part with real consequences attached, so it gets its own section. Phase 1 of the onboarding change says it plainly: week 1 behind is a redirect, week 2 behind is a formal warning and a keep-or-go decision. That ladder only works if the check-in is carrying the paper. Right now, mostly, it is not.

WHAT WE WENT LOOKING FOR

Across 348 completed check-ins and 1,740 manager answers, two mention a warning. Both were written by the same manager.

Not two per branch. Two in the company. And neither of them is a warning that was actually issued — one is a warning that may be issued if a bar is missed by a date, and the other is a manager explaining to a mover how warnings work at all.

Meanwhile, on the other side of the same app: 216 of our 275 movers in training have at least one lockout on record with coaching logged in Rippling. Nine of those 216 have a check-in that mentions it. 128 have a completed check-in that never brings it up, and 79 have a coaching record and no completed check-in at all.

Read those two paragraphs together and you have the problem. We are building two separate records about the same person — a disciplinary trail in Rippling and a conversation trail in the app — and they do not reference each other. If it ever matters, the question is always the same one: did they know? The check-in is the answer to that question, or there isn’t one.

The escalation ladder — what the check-in has to say at each step

Three steps. The only one most of us are writing today is step one, and usually without the date that makes it count.

1 Behind for the first time — a redirect, no warning. The expectations box says what to fix and by when. That is the whole step. It is also the step that makes the next one legitimate — a warning with no documented redirect in front of it is a warning nobody saw coming.
2 Still behind at the next check-in — state the consequence before it happens. Name the bar, name the date, name what happens if the date passes. The example below is the only one of these in the company and it is exactly right.
3 The warning gets issued — write that in the check-in. What was issued, the date it was issued, and what has to change to close it out. If it is only in Rippling, the app says this mover was fine and Rippling says he was warned. Zero check-ins in the company currently do this.
◆ STEP 2 DONE RIGHT — THE ONLY ONE IN THE COMPANY Stay engaged in your training dashboard. If there is no progress made (you are currently at 55%) by 9/11/26 a final written warning may be issued. WHY IT WORKS · a number he can check, a date he can count to, and the consequence named out loud before it arrives. Nothing in that sentence is a surprise later. CREDIT Khevan Dueck · McKinney — written to Isaac on 9/4.

STEP 3 — WHAT IT SHOULD LOOK LIKE, BECAUSE NOBODY HAS WRITTEN ONE YET

  • Final written warning issued in Rippling on 9/12 for training progress. You were at 55% on 9/4 with a 9/11 date on it and finished at 61%. To close this out: Stage 4 complete by 9/26, and I need to see a task a day between now and then. We will look at it again at your next check-in.
  • What that sentence does: it names what was issued and when, ties it back to the bar and the date the mover already had in writing, and gives him a way out with a deadline on it. All three are things you would want in front of you if this ever became an unemployment question.

TWO THINGS ON US, NOT ON YOU

  • Warnings are being issued automatically off app lockouts, and some of them are wrong. That is not a rumor — it is in a check-in, from a manager, to a mover. Of the 216 movers carrying a lockout record, 182 have nothing but a bulk backfilled entry, which is not the same thing as a manager having had a conversation. Who double-checks an automatic warning before it counts is a question for HR and it is getting asked.
  • The lock moved to work days, not calendar days. That was the fix asked for in the 9/3 manager session and it has shipped — days off, RDOs and open trucks should stop triggering lockouts now. If you see one that still looks wrong, say so rather than coaching against it.

What we are fixing in the form

Some of this is on us, not on you. The report found something worth acting on: how well a field gets answered tracks almost exactly with how much guidance the box gives you.

The acknowledge box gets a worked example It is the only one of the five without an Ex. answer, and it is the worst performing at 63%. The three fields with worked examples all do better. Showing the finished answer is doing real work.
“This should never be left empty” goes on all five Right now it is only on expectations — the best-answered field in the app at 14% filler. One line of text, four more boxes.
A minimum answer length Today a single dash submits and locks the check-in as complete. That is how 131 N/A-class answers got in. A floor stops a bare score from counting.
The guidance should stay visible while you type The prompt and the example live in the placeholder, so they vanish the moment you type a character. You cannot re-read the example while writing the answer. That is a fixable annoyance, not a manager problem.

The Ex. lines we are proposing for the boxes

These are the model answers above, cut down to fit in the box as guidance. Four of the five fields already carry an Ex.; acknowledge carries none, and it is the worst field in the app. Square brackets are the part the manager fills in.

ACKNOWLEDGE THEIR FEEDBACK NEW — NO EXAMPLE TODAY
What did the mover ask for, and what are you doing about it? Answer the thing they raised. Ex. You said you need more support on long jobs. Here is what we are doing: [the answer]. If it happens again, [what they should do]. The prompt itself also needs rewriting. As written — Did the mover ask any questions or provide any feedback… — it reads as a yes/no question, which is why the honest answers in the app are Yes and transcriptions of what the mover said.
ATTENDANCE KEEP, TIGHTEN
Any attendance trends to address? Consistently late? Always calls out on weekends? Include their EMC Games attendance score. Ex. You are at X of 8 late points and X of 4 callout points, and your Games attendance score is X.X. [What you see in that.] [What changes going forward.] Three changes: the denominators, a what-changes clause, and the Games attendance score, which the box does not ask for today. That score is the one attendance number with a payout attached to it, and it belongs in the same conversation as the late points rather than in a separate one nobody has.
HANDBOOK PROGRESSION KEEP, TIGHTEN
Ex. You are at X% with a velocity of X tasks a day and [no lockouts / X lockouts] since we last talked. That puts you [ahead of / behind] pace. [The next specific step.]
PEER FEEDBACK KEEP, TIGHTEN
Ex. Asked [lead] and [crew member]. They said [what is working]. The one thing that came back was [what to tighten]. Naming who you asked is the whole fix here. It turns the box from a judgement into an errand, and an errand is harder to answer with N/A.
EXPECTATIONS GOING FORWARD ADD AN EX.
Lay out in plain english what they need to improve on in order to be considered one of the best on the team. This should never be left empty. Ex. [Behavior] — [specific next action]. Get [X] done by [date]. If that has not happened by then, [consequence]. This is already the best field at 14% filler on the strength of the never-leave-empty line alone. It has no worked example, and it is also the field where one manager in the company routinely writes a date. Both facts point the same way.

SO WE ARE CLEAR ON SEQUENCE

  • We are not going to train our way out of a form defect. The acknowledge field is the worst in the app and it is also the only one with no example — that is a design problem before it is a manager problem. Those four fixes are going to Mike.
  • And the standard on this page still applies now. Two thirds of movers are getting a score back today, and not one of these fixes is needed to write a real sentence in five boxes. The Games attendance score is already on a dashboard you open daily — nothing on this page is waiting on a build.
